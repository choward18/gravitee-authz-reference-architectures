Solution reference architecture

Access & Authorization Management: three reference architectures

How Gravitee delivers identity-aware, fine-grained authorization — as a full platform, alongside a subset of Gravitee products, or as a standalone authorization layer for a predominantly non-Gravitee estate.

01 Full Gravitee deployment 02 Authorization Management + selected Gravitee products 03 Authorization Management in a non-Gravitee landscape
Two complementary layers
Access Management · authentication

Who you are

Identity and authentication. OAuth 2.0 / OpenID Connect, enterprise federation, token exchange, consent, and agent identity (CIMD, SPIFFE/SPIRE) for humans, workloads and AI agents.

Authorization Management · authorization

What you can do

Fine-grained authorization. Catalog-aware policies (GAPL) authored against real entities, decided by a high-performance engine at the point of enforcement — for APIs, events, models, MCP tools and agents.

The building blocks you'll see in every diagram

PEP
Policy Enforcement Point

Where a decision is enforced — a gateway, app or sidecar in the request path.

PDP
Policy Decision Point

Where the decision is made. Runs embedded in the gateway, or standalone behind an AuthZEN decision API.

PAP
Policy Administration Point

Where policies are authored and managed — the Model Editor, Policy Editor and Playground.

PIP
Policy Information Point

Extra context the decision needs — e.g. data classification from Lexicon and connected data catalogs.

ARCHITECTURE 01

Full Gravitee deployment

One runtime for every traffic type. A single Gravitee Gateway — one application, one installation, one runtime — is the Policy Enforcement Point for REST/GraphQL/gRPC, Kafka and LLM/MCP/A2A traffic alike; there is no separate proxy to stand up per protocol. Access Management provides OAuth/OIDC identity; fine-grained authorization is authored as GAPL policy in the console and enforced in that gateway. Agent Management adds the Catalog, Agent Identity, lineage and an on-device Edge Daemon, over shared stores for analytics, quotas and OpenTelemetry traces.

When to use

Consolidating the estate

A single governance plane across APIs, events and AI — not bolted-together point tools.

Key trait

One gateway, every protocol

A single runtime handles REST/GraphQL/gRPC, Kafka and LLM/MCP/A2A traffic under one identity, policy and telemetry model — not a proxy per protocol.

Key trait

Agent-native

Catalog, Agent Identity (desktop/hosted/workload), lineage and on-device shadow-AI detection built in.

ARCHITECTURE 02

Authorization Management + selected Gravitee products

You don't need the whole platform to get fine-grained authorization. Run Authorization Management alongside the Gravitee products you already use — the decision engine embeds directly into your existing Gravitee gateway. Identity can come from Gravitee Access Management or from your existing identity provider. Add further product lines later on the same policy substrate.

When to use

Already on Gravitee APIM

You run Gravitee API Management and want to add real fine-grained authorization without new infrastructure.

When to use

Phased adoption

Start with authorization on one product line; extend to events and agents later without re-authoring.

Key trait

Bring your own identity

Pair with Gravitee Access Management or federate your existing Okta / Entra / Auth0 as the front door.

ARCHITECTURE 03

Authorization Management in a predominantly non-Gravitee landscape

Use Gravitee as the authorization brain for an estate you don't want to replace. Authorization Management runs as a standalone decision service exposed through an AuthZEN-compliant decision API: any conformant enforcement point — third-party gateways, custom applications, sidecars — calls it for allow/deny decisions. Policies are authored centrally and made data-aware via Lexicon and your existing data catalogs. This is the "centralized decisioning, federated information" pattern.

When to use

Heterogeneous estate

Many gateways and apps from different vendors — you want one place to author and decide authorization.

When to use

Standards-first

You've committed to the AuthZEN authorization standard and need a conformant, high-performance PDP.

Key trait

Data-aware policy

Lexicon binds your data catalog's classifications into decisions — mask, redact or deny on sensitive fields.

Interoperable, not all-or-nothing

The same Authorization Management control plane spans all three architectures. Start standalone in a non-Gravitee estate, add Gravitee gateways where they fit, and grow toward the full platform — without re-authoring policy. The decision engine simply moves from a standalone service to embedded in the gateway as adoption deepens.
